1. My therapist said to set boundaries, so I built a fence, hired a guard named Dennis, and now Dennis charges me $40 to visit my own feelings.
2. I asked my smart fridge to judge my life choices; it locked itself, ordered 14 tubs of ice cream, and left a note: "Intervention's in the freezer."
3. My dad finally learned to text: first message "call me", second "ignore that", third a photo of his ear — he's been on speakerphone with his pocket since 2019.
4. I bought a plant to prove I could keep something alive; it died in 9 days, but its pot now holds my keys, my spare change, and all the confidence I used to lend it.
5. My alarm and I have an arrangement: it rings at 6, I lie at 6:07, and by 6:13 we're both pretending the meeting moved.
6. My cat brought me a dead mouse on Monday, a live lizard on Tuesday, and yesterday my own spare house key — I think she's interviewing replacements.
7. I taught my parrot to say "per my last email"; now he runs the office, I'm Cc'd on my own performance review, and Gary from accounts cries on Thursdays.
8. I told Grandma her photos live in the cloud; she now dresses up for thunderstorms, and last week she asked a cumulonimbus why it deleted Uncle Ray.
9. My dentist found 3 cavities, a popcorn kernel from 2022, and — his words — "a small but determined shrimp"; I haven't eaten shrimp since the wedding.
10. My sleep tracker scored me 41, suggested I "try being unconscious", and this morning it filed a restraining order against my phone.
11. I put "proficient in Excel" on my résumé; the spreadsheet believed me, promoted itself to manager, and now reviews my performance every Friday at 4:47.
12. My mum asked the voice assistant to find her glasses; it found them on her head, rated her search history 2 stars, and now answers only to "Detective".
13. My landlord said the apartment has "character"; the character is a raccoon named Biscuit who pays no rent, knows the Wi-Fi password, and has seen me cry.
14. I started meditating to silence my inner critic; now he whispers, he has a podcast, and 40,000 people agree with him about my haircut.
15. I named my houseplant Kevin so I'd feel too guilty to kill it; Kevin died in March, but the guilt moved into his pot and put out three new leaves.
16. My GPS and I are in couples therapy: I never listen, it never forgives, and last night it recalculated our relationship into a U-turn.
17. I asked Dad to water my plants for a week; he built them a schedule, a WhatsApp group, and a small fence — the tomatoes now call him Captain.
18. Grandad refused a smartphone for 30 years, got one in March, and by April he was selling the family group chat a timeshare.

## Tournament notes (round 1)
- Quarter-finals: 6 beats 2 (tighter, warmer turn); 7 beats 11 (voice stronger); 13 beats 9 (Biscuit is a character, shrimp is a gag); 15 beats 4 (same plant premise, 15 is 60 chars shorter and the twist re-reads the setup: guilt itself grows); 14 beats 10; 5 beats 16.
- Semi-finals: 15 beats 6 — cat line's turn is a guess ("I think"), Kevin's turn is an event; 15 beats 13 — raccoon needs its middle list, Kevin lands in two beats; 15 beats 14 — podcast is a strong second twist but 14's ending is a popularity gag, Kevin's ending is the plant world answering back.
- Delete-the-ending test on 15: without "and put out three new leaves" the line stops at a metaphor; with it, the guilt is literally growing — final beat is load-bearing. PASS.
- WINNER (pending final trim): #15.

## Final (round 2, stop-slop pass)
Shipped line: "I named my houseplant Kevin so I'd feel too guilty to kill it; Kevin died in March, but the guilt moved into his pot and put out three new leaves."
- Trim check: every clause earns its place — name+guilt (setup), died in March (odd specific, escalation), guilt grows leaves (twist that re-reads both halves). No filler adjectives, no explaining the joke.
- Second-read check: the joke improves — the guilt is the only thing the narrator ever kept alive, and it is now a plant.
